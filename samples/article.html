How UPI changed everyday payments

Published 14 October 2025 · 6 min read

The Unified Payments Interface launched in 2016, built by the National Payments Corporation of India under the guidance of the Reserve Bank of India. Within eight years it was processing more than 13 billion transactions a month.

Its design separates the payment address from the bank account. A user shares a virtual payment address instead of an account number and IFSC code, which reduced errors and fraud for small merchants.

Adoption grew fastest in 2020, when contactless payments became a public-health measure. Smaller cities such as Indore and Coimbatore saw growth of over 40 percent that year.

Version 2.0 of the protocol added overdraft accounts, mandates for recurring payments and invoice previews. Analysts expect cross-border links with Singapore and the UAE to drive the next phase.
